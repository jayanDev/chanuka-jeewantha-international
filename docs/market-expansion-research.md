# Thirty additional country websites

Research and implementation date: 10 September 2026.

## Selection and limits

This expands the existing 20 country editions to 50. The additional markets were selected for their fit with international professional mobility, English-language applications, specialist and executive career branding, and visible CV/LinkedIn service-provider ecosystems. The selection spans 15 European, eight Asian, four African and three American markets.

These are priority expansion candidates, not a statistically verified list of the world's 30 highest-demand CV markets. No paid keyword-volume dataset, country-local rank tracker, competitor traffic account or AI citation-monitoring dataset was available. Public search results and providers' own service pages establish relevant service intent, not market size, conversion demand, first-place rankings or AEO/GEO leadership. The [OECD International Migration Outlook](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/international-migration-outlook-2025_ae26c893-en.html) supplies broader mobility context, not demand measurements for paid CV writing.

Initial commercial validation should distinguish established international-professional markets (for example Germany, Netherlands, Switzerland and Luxembourg) from language-sensitive markets (Japan, South Korea, France and Latin America) and cross-border career markets (for example Philippines, Pakistan, Nepal and the African additions). This is an editorial prioritisation hypothesis, not a revenue forecast. Validate enquiry quality, conversion and search queries before increasing spend.

## How the analysis was applied

- Competitors commonly combine CV/resume, LinkedIn, cover-letter and career guidance services. The site keeps its approved six premium services and five international bundles rather than importing competitors' low-price tiers.
- Country keywords appear naturally in introductions, service positioning and substantive article paragraphs, as well as titles/descriptions. Metadata keyword arrays are not treated as a Google ranking mechanism.
- Each new market has original application guidance, LinkedIn advice, strategy paragraphs, a practical evidence example and a country-specific FAQ. Names, prices and city lists alone are not the content differentiation.
- Each market has home, about, contact, services, catalogue and blog pages plus three articles: `/blog/cv-guide`, `/blog/linkedin-profile`, `/blog/career-strategy`. The expansion adds 270 URLs, including 90 articles; all 50 markets have 450 country URLs in total.
- Founder-led identity and English remote delivery stay explicit. No invented office, local registration, certification, customer testimonial, job outcome or AI-search endorsement was added.
- Article answers, descriptive headings, source links, author identity and appropriate WebPage, Service, BreadcrumbList and BlogPosting structured data help readers and machine interpretation. They are not a promise of AI citation or ranking.

Google's [spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) make doorway and low-value scaled content a relevant risk for country expansion. The shared layout is intentional; country guidance and examples must continue to earn their place. Review actual user questions and merge or improve underperforming pages instead of generating more near-duplicate location variants. Follow [Google's generative AI guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide) rather than treating an AEO/GEO label as a shortcut.

## Currency and publication policy

The approved USD service and bundle amounts remain the base prices. Country pages display rounded, explicitly labelled reference estimates, not a checkout commitment or an assurance that a payment provider operates in that country. Added 21 currency records; nine new markets reuse EUR. BHD retains three fractional digits; JPY and KRW are displayed without fractional digits.

Rates were checked against [ExchangeRate-API's open USD feed](https://open.er-api.com/v6/latest/USD), whose returned snapshot was 9 September 2026 at 00:02:31 UTC. The site preserves that reference date and source attribution. New articles are dated 10 September; the original 20 markets retain their 9 September publication dates.

- `src/lib/markets.ts`: 50-country registry, route generation and reciprocal hreflang.
- `src/content/additional-market-editorial.ts`: 30 original country content records and publication date.
- `src/content/market-editorial.ts`: existing and new content registry.
- `src/lib/market-rates.ts`, `market-pricing.ts`: reference conversions and currency precision.
- `src/lib/market-articles.ts`, `market-seo.ts`, `CountryPages.tsx`: publication dates, articles, metadata and schema.
- `src/components/markets/CountryDirectory.tsx`, `country.module.css`, `src/app/international/page.tsx`: searchable 50-market directory; all links exist in initial rendered HTML.
- Existing `src/app/sitemap.ts`, `robots.ts` and dynamic country routes consume the registry, so new URLs are included without another hardcoded route list.
- Unit tests and `scripts/verify-country-sites.cjs`: all-market content, prices, reciprocal links, currency precision, country search and desktop/mobile verification.

## Verification

Production build passed, including generation of 739 entries. All 95 unit tests and typecheck passed. Lint reported zero errors and three pre-existing unused-import warnings. Production HTTP checks passed all 450 country routes, their metadata, currencies, internal links and sitemap inclusion. Browser QA passed 120 desktop/mobile viewport checks, including 60 new-country hero checks with decoded images, country switching, directory search and intercepted enquiry submissions. No horizontal overflow or browser runtime failures were found in those checks.

The final full sitemap audit passed all 984 URLs with zero failures and zero retries. An earlier run had one transport-level fetch failure for an existing ATS-format blog article; three isolated requests returned 200 with a correct canonical and indexable metadata, and the full repeat crawl passed. The audit now retains transport-error details and permits one transport retry; it does not retry or hide HTTP-status or SEO failures. Missing-route checks also passed 26 real 404s, two single-hop redirects and stale-cookie sign-in. Reports and screenshots are under ignored `test-results/`.
